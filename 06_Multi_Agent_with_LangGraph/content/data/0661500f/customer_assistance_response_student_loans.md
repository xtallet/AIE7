Student loans are critical in enabling low-income students to access higher education, often acting as a necessary financial lifeline when other funding options are not feasible. According to the U.S. Department of Education, various loan programs exist specifically for students with financial need, such as subsidized loans, which offer favorable terms and lower interest rates compared to unsubsidized loans.

As of October 2023, following the cessation of COVID-19 emergency relief measures, federal student loan payments have resumed. While this presents challenges for many low-income borrowers, programs like Income-Driven Repayment (IDR) play a vital role in making repayments more manageable by tying them to income levels. These plans are designed to ensure payments remain affordable, helping to alleviate the financial burden on graduates entering the workforce.

Additionally, completing the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) is essential as it provides access to various forms of federal aid, including grants that do not require repayment. Importantly, there are no income restrictions on filling out the FAFSA, allowing all students, regardless of their financial situation, to apply for financial assistance.
