For the 2024 academic year, here are some key student loan policies designed to support low-income students:

1. **Subsidized Student Loans**: Dependent undergraduates can access up to $5,500 in total loans, which includes a portion available as subsidized loans. For the 2024 academic year, the maximum available in subsidized loans is $3,500. These loans do not accrue interest while the student is enrolled at least half-time, helping to alleviate financial burdens.

2. **Income-Based Repayment (IBR) Plan**: This plan offers flexible repayment options based on the borrower's income. It is particularly beneficial for low-income borrowers, allowing them to pay a manageable percentage of their income towards their loan and helping them navigate their early careers without overwhelming debt.

3. **Income-Contingent Repayment (ICR) Plan**: Similar to the IBR, this plan provides payment flexibility based on earnings, ensuring that borrowers are not overly burdened during repayment.

4. **Additional Loan Options**: Students progressing to higher grade levels during the same academic year may receive additional loan amounts if they qualify for higher limits based on their new academic status.

5. **Federal Student Aid Programs**: The federal government offers various aid programs that can further support low-income students, including Pell Grants which do not require repayment and thus reduce the overall need for borrowing.

For more detailed information and resources, students and borrowers can visit the [Federal Student Aid Website](https://studentaid.gov). This site provides comprehensive insights into student loans, repayment options, and financial support for low-income borrowers.
